# Pilot 014 — C2Y / Seoul National University Technology Holdings + KRISS (R&BD, Brown Field Sub-Type — University-Backed Active Co-Research)

**Case type**: R&BD (Brown Field sub-type, `case_subtype: rbd`) — a fourth real R&BD shape, distinct from all three prior pilots: a **university technology-holding-company equity investment** combined with an **ongoing joint-research relationship** (not a completed one-time technology transfer) with a government-funded research institute
**Region**: Korea (KR)
**Run mode**: Manual (no system — Stage 3/4 pilot per ADR-0034)
**Case source**: researched per user instruction (2026-08-20) to find a genuinely different R&BD shape beyond the three already piloted
**Status**: In progress — public disclosure is narrative-level (investor names, relationship facts) without specific transaction amounts

## 0. Purpose and Scope

Same public-data-only constraint as prior pilots. Pilots 011/012 are internal-venture spin-offs (`internal_only`/`accelerator_partnered`); pilot 013 is a completed, one-time technology license-in (`external_technology_acquisition`). This case is different from all three in two ways at once: (1) the equity investor base includes a **university technology holding company** (기술지주회사) alongside banks and corporate/independent VCs — a financing-structure element none of the prior three had; (2) the government-institute relationship (with KRISS, the Korea Research Institute of Standards and Science) is an **active, ongoing joint-research collaboration** for measurement standardization, not a completed transfer of pre-existing IP.

## 3. New Finding: R&BD Cases Are Not Always Structured as a Discrete "Deal"

Every prior pilot (001-013) examined a discrete transaction with an identifiable acquirer, target, and (usually) a disclosed or estimable deal value. This case is different: C2Y is not being acquired, licensed from, or spun off from anyone in this pilot's frame — it is an independently founded company whose R&BD-relevant facts (university-holdco equity, active government-institute co-research) describe its **own capital structure and technology-development relationships**, not a transaction this review team would evaluate as a Green/Brown Field investment thesis in the traditional sense. This matters for scoping: `checklists/rbd-technology-checklist.yaml` was designed assuming the reviewing entity is evaluating whether *to acquire/license/absorb* an R&BD target (as in pilots 011-013) — but a reviewing entity might instead be evaluating whether *to invest in* (take a minority stake in) a company like C2Y, which is a different transaction shape (closer to a venture-investment thesis than an M&A thesis), even though the underlying R&BD risk factors (TRL, IP ownership, government relationships) are the same ones the checklist already covers.

## 4. Findings

| # | Finding | Type | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| F-1 | rbd-02's wording doesn't distinguish a formal government co-funding grant (with technology-fee/IP conditions, as ETRI's programs have) from an informal or in-kind research collaboration with a government institute (as this case's KRISS relationship may be) — the DD implications are different (formal grants carry real, binding conditions; informal collaborations may not). | Gap — checklist wording | Broaden rbd-02 to explicitly require classifying a government-institute relationship as formal-co-funded vs. informal-collaboration before assessing conditions |
| F-2 | rbd-03 (FTO/IP ownership) doesn't address **joint IP ownership arising from active co-research**, only pre-existing IP being licensed or owned outright — a materially different diligence question (who owns jointly-developed IP, and under what terms) than a simple license-in chain. | Gap — checklist wording | Add explicit joint-IP-ownership guidance to rbd-03 for cases involving active co-development with an external research partner |
| F-3 | rbd-04's build/license/spin-off framing doesn't fit an independently-founded, university/government-research-backed startup that was never anyone's internal venture or licensee in the first place — the decision this checklist item asks about (how the *reviewing entity* wants to obtain the technology) still applies, but the *target's own origin* doesn't fit any of the three named paths. | Gap — checklist scope clarification | Clarify that rbd-04 concerns the reviewing entity's chosen path to the technology (build/license/invest/acquire), not the target's own founding history — the target's origin is captured separately via `rbd_source` |
| F-4 | This case surfaces a transaction-shape distinction not previously named: an R&BD review can be a **minority venture-investment thesis**, not just an acquisition/license/absorption thesis — the underlying risk checklist items still apply, but the deal-structure-hypothesis step should recognize minority venture investment as a valid R&BD deal shape alongside acquisition, license, and spin-off absorption. | Gap — procedure scope | Consider whether `brownfield-pre-fs`'s R&BD structure-hypothesis note should explicitly list "minority venture investment" alongside "internal-venture spin-off carve-out" as a real, distinct R&BD deal shape |
| F-5 | The NTU Singapore milestone is a real, well-corroborated, checkable track-record data point for an early-stage R&BD target — confirms rbd-06 can be satisfied even for a company much earlier-stage than pilots 011/012's targets, provided a specific, named, external milestone exists. | Positive — rbd-06 validated at the early-stage end of the spectrum | None needed |

## 5. Verdict

**Process partially executable, and this pilot's primary value is structural rather than confirmatory** — it surfaced four real gaps (F-1 through F-4) that only a case shaped this differently from pilots 011-013 could have revealed: the formal-vs-informal government relationship distinction, joint-IP-ownership diligence, the build/license/spin-off framing's scope limit, and the minority-venture-investment deal shape. Not a failure under ADR-0034 — this is exactly the kind of genuinely novel real-case finding the pilot process exists to surface, and it came specifically from deliberately seeking a structurally different case rather than a fourth variation on the same theme.
