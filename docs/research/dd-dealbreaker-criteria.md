# Research Note — Deal Breaker and Covenant Waiver Standard Practices

> 🌐 External reference material. Researched: 2026-09-05. For comparing industry practices in the design of
> kill criteria (binding stop criteria) and waivers (exception approvals).

## Standard Practices — Red Flags That Kill a Deal

1. **Financial**: if three years of financial statements/tax filings do not reconcile with each other,
   that is not a due diligence gap but a signal of poor bookkeeping or concealment — demand structural
   protections or grounds for withdrawal (Bowery Legal)
2. **Behavioral·trust**: when management cannot explain basic metrics or repeatedly provides conflicting
   financial materials, it leads to deal termination or strong indemnity demands — the signal lies in
   "how the problem was handled" more than in "the problem itself"
3. **Legal**: non-assignable contract clauses (cannot be transferred without third-party consent) are a
   warning signal for renegotiation risk
4. **Decision framework**: if a risk can be clearly scoped, priced, and handled contractually (indemnity·
   escrow·price adjustment), renegotiate; if it is an unquantifiable black box, walk away —
   **materiality** and **quantifiability** are the key judgment axes

## Standard Practices — Covenant Waivers (financial loan agreement exceptions)

1. **Nature of a waiver**: a temporary forgiveness of a specific breach — four types: prospective /
   retrospective / limited (specific breach·time-bounded) / conditional (subject to fees·additional
   collateral)
2. **Why lenders approve waivers**: when the difficulty is temporary rather than a fundamental impairment
   of creditworthiness, and when maintaining the relationship is more advantageous than the cost and time
   of refinancing new debt — a formal request and review process is followed
3. **Documentation principle**: a waiver must explicitly state that it is **limited to the single breach
   in question**; repeatedly overlooking breaches risks being interpreted as an "implied amendment by
   course of dealing," under which all future breaches could be deemed waived — this is the core risk in
   waiver design: "an exception repeated is no longer a rule"

## co-newbiz mapping

- The co-newbiz rule "mandatory items that cannot be waived = KILL" is the same dichotomy as the industry
  framework of "material + unquantifiable = walk away."
- The waiver documentation principle (reason·approver·expiry date mandatory, no exceptions) maps directly
  to the covenant waiver "limited to a single breach, explicitly stated" principle — it structurally blocks
  the industry failure mode of degrading into an "implicit standing waiver" when no expiry date exists.
