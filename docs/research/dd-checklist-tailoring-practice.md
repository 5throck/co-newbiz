# Research Note — Industry-Specific DD Checklist Tailoring Standard Practices

> 🌐 External reference material. Researched: 2026-09-05. For comparing industry practices in the design of ADR-0099 tailored checklists (external search benchmarking).

## Standard Practices

1. **A standard checklist is a starting point, not an endpoint**: while core principles are common, each
   industry has its own unique risks, regulations, and valuation variables, so due diligence must be
   tailored to the realities of the target industry (Diligent, Chambers)
2. **Examples of industry-specific focus**: technology (IP ownership·data security·SOC2/ISO27001
   certifications·retention), manufacturing (environmental liability·equipment condition·supply chain),
   healthcare (licenses·reimbursement systems), startups (cap table·burn rate·contractor-developed IP)
3. **Multidisciplinary team composition**: only a multidisciplinary team with financial, legal, operational,
   and industry expertise can fill out a checklist into a comprehensive risk assessment — a single
   perspective misses industry-specific risks
4. **Item-by-item necessity review**: customize by asking "Is this item decisive for this particular deal?"
   of each standard item, and also adjust according to deal structure, e.g. asset purchase vs equity
   purchase
5. **Continuous updating**: regularly refreshing the checklist to reflect market conditions, regulatory
   changes, and insights from past due diligence is recommended practice — it is not a one-time static
   document

## co-newbiz mapping

- ADR-0099's principle of "keeping standard items as the authoritative original while custom items
  augment" exactly matches the industry practice of "layering industry risks on top of a standard
  starting point."
- Requiring a source-reliability grade (A/B/C) on custom items is a co-newbiz-specific extension that
  substitutes the industry-recommended "multidisciplinary expert verification" with a grading system
  instead of a single responsible person.
