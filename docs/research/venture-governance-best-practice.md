## AREA A — Early Strategic-Fit Screening (before heavy analysis)

### A.1 What the published models actually do

- **Cooper's Stage-Gate puts strategic fit at Gate 1, not at final approval.** Gate 1 (Idea Screen) is the first resource-committing decision ("the project is born at this point"): a *gentle* screen using **must-meet "knock-out" criteria** (strategic alignment, feasibility, no safety/environmental/regulatory showstoppers) plus **should-meet criteria rated 0–10** (strategic alignment, market attractiveness, synergy with operations, reward vs. risk), evaluated on a visible scorecard by a small cross-functional group of mid-level managers with authority to commit resources on the spot; rejected ideas get written feedback (Stage-Gate Institute — https://www.stage-gate.com/blog/optimizing-the-stage-gate-process-what-best-practice-companies-are-doing/; Cooper, PDMA 2026 — https://community.pdma.org/blogs/robert-cooper/2026/02/03/stage-gate-the-official-2026-version).
- **Gate 2 (Second Screen) re-tests strategic fit and synergy before the business-case build**, so no project enters heavy analysis without passing strategic filters twice (Stage-Gate Institute — https://www.stage-gate.com/blog/the-stage-gate-model-an-overview/).
- **M&A best practice is a standing screen with pre-declared "hunt zones"** derived from strategy, screening targets on strategy-fit criteria before relationship/deal work begins — screening is a systematic capability, not a per-deal afterthought (Bain & Company — https://www.bain.com/insights/ma-screening-global-ma-report-2020/; practitioner operationalization — https://umbrex.com/resources/corporate-development-primer/ma-strategy-and-target-screening/).
- **A published scorecard format exists**: one-page acquisition-fit scorecard, 1–5 ratings across strategic / financial / operational / cultural fit dimensions (Dealmaker Wealth Society — https://dealmakerwealthsociety.com/deal-sourcing/benefits-strategic-partnerships-acquisitions/strategic-fit-analysis-business-mergers/).
- **Public-sector equivalent — HM Treasury Green Book Five Case Model**: the **Strategic Case comes first**; the **Strategic Outline Case (SOC)** is the earliest approval gate — it establishes the *rationale for intervention* (case for change), tests each option's **fit with the strategic context and stated priorities**, and produces a **longlist → shortlist** BEFORE full economic appraisal (HM Treasury Green Book 2026 — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book-appraisal-and-evaluation-in-central-government/the-green-book-2026; SOC template (GOV.UK) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5fb2600de90e0709e40951d5/Template_for_Strategic_Outline_Case__002_.odt; gov.scot Five Case Model — https://www.gov.scot/publications/principles-developing-business-case/pages/2/).
- **Right-to-win is a standard first-screen axis**: the market-attractiveness × ability-to-win (right-to-win) two-axis matrix (GE/McKinsey nine-box lineage) is the standard portfolio screen; e.g., Kraft Heinz publicly states growth platforms are selected on "high market attractiveness AND right to win" (Strategy Punk — https://www.strategypunk.com/strategy-frameworks/; StrategyU — https://strategyu.co/market-entry-framework/; Kraft Heinz IR — https://ir.kraftheinzcompany.com/company-overview/company-strategy).

### A.2 Strategic-Fit Screening Checklist (run at first screen, BEFORE heavy analysis)

**Scoring structure (from Cooper/Green Book practice):** Part 1 = must-meet knock-outs (Pass/Fail — any Fail terminates or recycles, no further analysis). Part 2 = rated items (0–10, weighted; total threshold e.g. ≥60/100 to advance, 45–59 Hold/Recycle with named gaps, <45 Kill). Evidence allowed: desk research and the sponsor's own knowledge only — no commissioned analysis.

**Part 1 — Must-meet knock-out criteria (Yes/No; any "No" = reject at screen)**
1. **Strategic alignment —** the opportunity advances at least one named objective in the current corporate/business-unit strategy, cited by name.
2. **Portfolio / hunt-zone fit —** the opportunity falls within a pre-declared investment thesis area ("hunt zone"), or carries an explicit exception approval from the portfolio owner.
3. **No disqualifying constraint —** no known fatal safety, environmental, regulatory, sanctions, or reputational barrier.
4. **Strategic intent category declared —** the opportunity is classified as core-enhancement / adjacent-expansion / transformational bet, and that category's risk tolerance is acknowledged by the sponsor.
5. **Named executive sponsor —** one named executive owns the opportunity through the next gate and controls the seed budget.

**Part 2 — Should-meet rated criteria (0–10 each, weighted)**
6. **Preliminary market attractiveness —** estimated market size/growth passes the floor set for the declared intent category (desk research only at this gate).
7. **Right-to-win hypothesis —** at least one articulable advantage the company brings (asset, capability, channel, brand, data, license) that a generic entrant lacks.
8. **Synergy thesis —** identifiable synergies with existing operations (channels, plants, technology, customer base) beyond standalone value.
9. **Capability-fit gap statement —** required vs. current capabilities assessed; every material gap is named with a plausible source (build / partner / buy).
10. **Preliminary feasibility —** no known insurmountable technical or operational obstacle; rough time-to-market acceptable for the category.
11. **Reward-vs-risk scale —** preliminary size of the prize is material relative to the effort and risk, per category thresholds.
12. **Options discipline (Green Book) —** the proposal is one of at least two routes to the stated objective, and a do-minimum/status-quo baseline exists for comparison.

**Gate hygiene rules (from Cooper's Gate 1 design):** scorecard criteria are visible to submitters; decisions are Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle only; rejected ideas enter an idea vault with written feedback; the screen runs on a fixed cadence (monthly/bimonthly) with gatekeepers empowered to commit resources on the spot.

## AREA B — Internal Venture / R&BD / New-Business Establishment Governance

### B.1 Stage-gate for new business development (Cooper, generations 1→5)

- **Canonical structure:** Discovery/Ideation → Gate 1 Idea Screen → Stage 1 Concept (quick scoping, desk research) → Gate 2 Second Screen → Stage 2 Build the Business Case → Gate 3 Go to Development → Stage 3 Development → Gate 4 → Stage 4 Testing & Validation → Gate 5 → Stage 5 Launch → post-launch review (Stage-Gate Institute — https://www.stage-gate.com/blog/the-stage-gate-model-an-overview/).
- **What the business-case gate demands** (output of Stage 2, judged at Gate 3; "Go to Development"): (a) firm product/venture **definition**; (b) **market evidence** from primary research / voice-of-customer, market sizing, positioning, pricing, revenue forecast; (c) **technical feasibility** results; (d) **financial evaluation** — risk-adjusted, e.g. Expected Commercial Value; (e) **risk assessment**; (f) **project plan** with timeline, resources, and next-stage deliverables. Gate 3 is the last cheap kill point before major spend (Stage-Gate Institute overview; Cooper, PDMA 2026; Cooper & Edgett — https://bptrends.info/wp-content/publicationfiles/07-06-ART-StageGateForProductDev-Cooper-Edgett1.pdf).
- **Gate architecture = deliverables + criteria + outputs.** Criteria judge **project readiness** AND **project value** (Value-Based Scorecard; Expected Commercial Value). Outputs are **Go / Kill / Hold / Recycle**; every Go must confirm **resourcing (budget, headcount) and set the next gate date** (Stage-Gate Institute; PDMA 2026).
- **Gatekeepers scale with the gate:** early gates = cross-functional senior/mid managers with commit authority; Gates 3–5 of major projects = business leadership team (Stage-Gate Institute).
- **Generations:** 3rd gen = faster/flexible (fast-track versions, overlapping stages, conditional go) (Cooper 1994 JPIM — https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Robert-Cooper-40/publication/247645321_Third-Generation_New_Product_Processes/links/5cc053f34185156cd7aea56c/Third-Generation-New-Product-Processes.pdf); 4th/5th gen = agile-in-stage, adaptive/iterative, "right-sized for context and risk" (Stage-Gate Institute — https://www.stage-gate.com/about/5th-generation-stage-gate-model/).

### B.2 Corporate venturing governance

- **Vehicle taxonomy (choose deliberately):** single direct → multiple direct → portfolio → LP in fund → own GP/LP fund → co-GP fund (Sifted — https://sifted.eu/articles/how-to-pick-cvc-model); direct balance-sheet holding vs. ring-fenced vehicle trade-offs (Osborne Clarke — https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/); CVC units are structurally diverse, so **decision rights must be designed explicitly, not inherited from VC defaults** (Stanford GSB working paper — https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/working-papers).
- **Venture-building governance:** CEO-level priority setting, staged investment discipline, dedicated structures; serial builders outperform (McKinsey — https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-ceos-critical-role-in-building-new-businesses and https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-way-to-win-in-corporate-venturing-serial-building-and-ai).
- **Internal ventures are governed by a "venture board"** acting as a milestone-gated funding layer around the venture team — tranches released against milestones rather than annual budget (Bundl — https://www.bundl.com/knowledge/guides/strategy-corporate-venturing-the-ultimate-guide; venture-board literature — https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sej.1258).

### B.3 Legal-entity formation checklist for a new subsidiary/venture (internal-origin project)

*Synthesized from law-firm published checklists (DLA Piper pre/post-incorporation — https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/accelerate/formation/aiming-to-start-off-right-pre-and-post-incorporation-checklists; Robinson+Cole employee IP assignment — https://www.rfemerge.com/2021/03/26/employee-assignments-for-emerging-companies-key-considerations/).*

**Pre-incorporation**
1. Jurisdiction and entity type selected, with tax/regulatory rationale documented and approved.
2. Name availability cleared with the registry; preliminary trademark clearance completed.
3. Share structure and parent/founder allocations fixed; initial directors and officers identified.
4. Originating parent's internal approvals obtained (board/GM resolution authorizing formation, funding, and IP contribution).

**Formation and legal wiring**
5. Charter/articles filed; registered address and agent appointed; registered capital paid in.
6. Organizational documents executed: bylaws, incorporator action, board consents, director indemnification agreements.
7. **IP assignment into the entity:** all pre-formation IP (parent background IP and originating team's work) assigned or licensed in writing (IP assignment / bill of sale); every employee and contractor signs a proprietary-information-and-inventions assignment (PIIA) agreement.
8. **People transfer:** new employment agreements + PIIAs signed; where staff come from the parent, a secondment or transfer agreement per applicable consent rules (see B.5 for Korea).
9. **Intra-group agreements executed:** intra-group services agreement; IP/brand license; supply or distribution agreements; cost-sharing/transfer-pricing policy documented.
10. Statutory registrations completed: tax IDs, employer registrations, business licenses, sector permits; foreign-investment filings if cross-border.
11. Banking, insurance, and accounting/audit arrangements opened; statutory books and registers established.
12. Governance baseline set: first board minutes, delegated-authority matrix, compliance calendar; data room opened containing all of the above.

### B.4 JV governance checklist (esp. true 50/50)

*Sources: Umbrex JV Governance Architecture — https://umbrex.com/resources/joint-venture-playbook/jv-governance-architecture/; Global Law Experts deadlock mechanisms — https://globallawexperts.com/how-do-5050-joint-ventures-avoid-deadlock/; LexisNexis — https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/deadlock-in-corporate-joint-ventures.*

1. Board of **5–9 members**; equal seats in a 50/50, with deadlock design (independent chair or casting vote or rotating chair) agreed at formation.
2. **Reserved-matters list requiring mutual consent:** strategy/scope changes; annual plan and budget; major capex and financing; CEO/CFO appointment and removal; material related-party transactions; acquisitions/divestitures; changes to constitutional documents; ownership economics and transfer rules; dissolution. Keep the list narrow, category-based, and threshold-driven.
3. **Deadlock ladder with binding deadlines:** management reconciliation → board decision memo → shareholder principals empowered to decide → cooling-off/standstill (45–60 days; JV operates on last-approved budget) → expert determination for defined factual/valuation issues (30–60 days) → mediation → final remedy.
4. **Exit clauses:** put/call with pre-agreed valuation formula (EBITDA multiple / DCF / NAV); Russian-roulette or Texas shoot-out as last resort; ROFR/ROFO and tag/drag rights; termination events; 60–90-day completion mechanics including regulatory approvals.
5. Standing committees (few and chartered): audit & risk; compensation & nomination; related-party/conflicts.
6. **One-voice management:** direction to the venture only via board decisions and the CEO; written delegated-authority matrix with variance bands and emergency limits.
7. Policy library adopted (pricing, credit, procurement, delegation of authority, data access, compliance, information-handling where parents compete), reviewed at least annually.
8. Firewall/clean-team protocols where the parents compete in adjacent markets.
9. Reporting rhythm: monthly management report vs. plan, quarterly board meetings, event-based reporting for material incidents; "constructive transparency" norm (early bad news rewarded).

### B.5 Korea specifics (public sources)

- **Company split (인적분할/물적분할) — Commercial Act arts. 527 et seq.:** requires a **shareholders' special resolution (art. 527-2)** and the **creditor-protection procedure (art. 527-5: individual notice to known creditors plus public notice, objection window of at least one month)** (English text — Korea Legislation Research Institute: https://elaw.klri.re.kr/eng_service/lawView.do?hseq=54525&lang=ENG). **2025 amendments** restrict "spin-off magic" share-issuance dilution and permit non-share consideration (Yoon & Yang — https://www.yoonyang.com/eng/insights/newsletter/2590?currentPage=1; Kim & Chang — https://www.kimchang.com/en/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=34499; ACGA — https://www.acga-asia.org/blog-detail.php?id=115; Lee & Ko on vertical (holdco owns 100% of newco) vs. horizontal splits — https://www.leeko.com/pdf/Article_SGK_9.pdf).
- **Employee transfer:** Korea has **no TUPE-style statute**; courts apply a **doctrine of automatic succession of employment on a business transfer** (substance over form — HR + organization + assets + goodwill): position, continuous service, wages, severance and other terms carry over; dismissal solely because of the transfer is unlawful; accrued employment liabilities are borne jointly by transferor and transferee; employees may object/refuse in practice, so individual consent plus term-harmonization is the safe path; pure share deals carry no statutory employee consent rights (Korean Law Blog — https://www.thekoreanlawblog.com/2018/10/employment-transfers-korea.html; Lexology M&A structures Korea — https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8f97c082-1dd0-415d-8e31-071b2c03a76c).
- **KFTC scrutiny of intra-group transactions:** the KFTC actively monitors intra-affiliate transactions for unfair support of controlling shareholders; intra-group supply/services need arm's-length pricing discipline (Kim & Chang — https://www.kimchang.com/en/expertise/view/antitrust-competition; ICLG Korea merger control — https://iclg.com/practice-areas/merger-control-laws-and-regulations/korea/).

## CROSS-CUTTING ANSWERS

**(1) At which gate should a venture prove "right to win" vs "market attractiveness"?**
Published models treat the two on different clocks. Market attractiveness is screened first and cheaply — at the very first screen (Cooper's Gate 1 rating items; the Green Book SOC's "is the opportunity worth pursuing at all" test) using desk research only — because it answers whether the space justifies any analysis spend. Right to win is *hypothesized* at the first screen (one articulable advantage) but must be *proven* at the business-case gate (Cooper's Gate 2 Second Screen re-tests "ability to leverage core competencies/synergy," and Gate 3 demands primary evidence) — i.e., before the first major resource commitment — because demonstrating right to win requires capability mapping, VOC evidence, and competitive analysis that cannot be done honestly at the front door. Screening order: attractiveness → in; right-to-win proof → through. Sources: StrategyU (https://strategyu.co/market-entry-framework/), The Compass (https://the-compass.io/p/from-fuzzy-to-fundable-a-new-way-to-value-innovation), Stage-Gate Institute, Kraft Heinz IR.

**(2) What governance controls do published models require when a venture is allowed to SKIP a stage (fast-track/waiver)?**
Published models do not waive gates — they shorten or overlap **stages** while keeping every **gate** mandatory. The required controls are: (a) **risk-based process right-sizing** — multiple pre-defined process versions (full for major/platform projects; an abbreviated fast-track for small, low-risk, well-defined projects), chosen at Gate 1, not negotiated mid-flight (Cooper 3rd-gen; "After Stage-Gate" — https://media.transformanceadvisors.com/pdfs/After-Stage-Gate.pdf); (b) **"Conditional Go" instead of waiver** — the project proceeds with named caveats, an expiry/re-verification date, and the obligation to close gaps before a later gate; (c) **no unfunded Go** — even fast-tracked projects get explicit resourcing and a next-gate date at each gate; (d) **stages may overlap, but every gate's deliverables must still exist** at review (quality control is the gate's purpose, and gatekeepers remain empowered senior people); and (e) **documented exception logging with feedback** — decisions and deviations are minuted, and killed/held items are stored in an idea vault rather than informally dropped. In short: skip stages by pre-declared risk class, never skip the decision point.
